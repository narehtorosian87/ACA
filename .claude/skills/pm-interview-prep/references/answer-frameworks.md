# Answer frameworks

Use these in step 5 of `SKILL.md` to structure draft answers. Personalize with real specifics from `resume.md` wherever possible — a framework filled with vague generalities is worse than no framework.

## Behavioral — STAR
- **Situation**: 1-2 sentences of real context (company/team/timeframe).
- **Task**: what specifically was the user responsible for.
- **Action**: what *they* did — first person, concrete, not "we decided" for the whole story.
- **Result**: quantified where possible (%, $, time saved, users affected). If no hard metric exists, use a concrete qualitative outcome instead of inventing a number.

Tip: strongest behavioral answers pick a story with real tension (disagreement, ambiguity, failure) rather than a clean success story with no conflict.

## Product sense / design — structured framework (CIRCLES-style)
1. **Clarify** the goal/user/constraints before answering.
2. **Identify** the target user segment.
3. **Report** that user's needs/pain points.
4. **Cut** through with a prioritization lens (impact vs. effort, or a stated principle).
5. **List** 2-3 concrete solutions.
6. **Evaluate** tradeoffs between them.
7. **Summarize** with a clear recommendation — always end on a decision, not just an analysis.

## Analytical / metrics
1. Clarify the question and what "success" means here.
2. Define the metric tree (a north star + the inputs that move it).
3. Form 2-3 hypotheses for what's driving the number.
4. Describe how you'd validate each (data pull, experiment, qualitative research).
5. Give a recommendation and what you'd watch next.

## Strategy / vision
1. Frame the question (what's actually being decided).
2. Look at it through 2-3 lenses: user, market/competitive, company capability.
3. Lay out the real options (including "don't do it").
4. Recommend one, with the key risk you're accepting.

## General rules
- Every answer should end with a recommendation or a result — panels remember conclusions, not analysis.
- Keep answers tight enough to say in 90 seconds to 2 minutes; note where the user should trim in practice.
- Where `resume.md` has a directly relevant project, name it and its real numbers instead of a generic hypothetical.
