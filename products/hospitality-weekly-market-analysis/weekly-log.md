### Hospitality technology & AI

1. **AI agents are rewriting corporate GDS hotel listings before travelers ever see them, and agent-to-agent negotiation could bypass OTAs entirely** — Hospitality Net (HN Brief), week of Sep 2026
   - Summary: AI-powered corporate booking tools (e.g., Concur, Navan) are already re-ranking and rewriting how hotel GDS listings appear to corporate travelers before a human sees them, and the same brief flags a near-term scenario where agent-to-agent negotiation could route bookings directly between buyer-side and hotel-side AI, skipping OTA intermediaries altogether. The same brief also notes LARC raised its 2026 U.S. RevPAR growth forecast to 5.1%.
   - Why it matters for Hotelchamp: This is the clearest early signal yet that "the booking engine's customer" may increasingly be another AI agent, not a human clicking through a funnel. Hotelchamp's booking engine should treat machine-readability (structured, agent-consumable rate/availability data — MCP-style interfaces) as a forward-looking roadmap item, not just human UX polish — being agent-visible could become a distribution channel in its own right, corporate travel first.

2. **Booking Holdings CEO: "scale still wins" once travel discovery moves to AI agents** — Skift, Sep 9, 2026 (Skift Global Forum 2026 preview coverage)
   - Summary: In Skift's curtain-raiser on the "five decisions" framing this year's Skift Global Forum, Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel is quoted arguing that even as AI agents take over travel discovery, scale (inventory breadth, data, brand trust) remains the deciding advantage — a direct rebuttal to the idea that agentic AI levels the playing field for smaller players.
   - Why it matters for Hotelchamp: This is a competitive-positioning signal worth internalizing: the largest OTAs are betting that scale wins the agentic era, which is exactly the axis Hotelchamp's direct-booking pitch has to counter for hotel clients — differentiation through owned guest data, rate control, and direct-channel loyalty, not inventory breadth. Useful framing for product marketing and for roadmap conversations about what a booking engine can offer that scale alone can't.
   - Source: [Skift Global Forum 2026: Five Decisions in the Room](https://skift.com/2026/09/09/skift-global-forum-2026-five-decisions-in-the-room/)

### So what this week
The clearest throughline is agentic AI moving from discovery-assistant to potential transaction intermediary — corporate GDS listings are already being reshaped by AI before humans see them, and both a neutral trade brief and Booking's own CEO are now talking openly about agents (not humans) as the next layer hotels have to be visible to. For Hotelchamp, the practical takeaway is to start scoping what "agent-visible, agent-transactable" means for the booking engine (structured data feeds, MCP-style interfaces) before it becomes table stakes, while on the market side, Choice's franchisee-growth push and Expedia's M&A-driven bundling edge over Booking are both worth monitoring for shifts in how hotel partners think about their distribution mix.
